## Where AI is genuinely useful in this job

- **Long-form synthesis.** Stitch together 12 customer interviews, 4 sales calls, and the latest data dump into a coherent doc. AI doesn't have the insight; you do. AI helps you not lose it in the noise.
- **PRDs and one-pagers from rough notes.** First-draft only. You edit hard.
- **User-story splitting.** "Help me break this into 3 stories that ship in 1 sprint each." The model is decent at slicing scope.
- **Competitive teardowns.** Paste a competitor's pricing page or feature list, ask for an honest analysis. Useful pressure-test of your assumptions.
- **Stakeholder messaging.** Different drafts of the same news (good or bad) for engineering vs leadership vs customers.

## Where AI is not the right tool

- **Deciding what to build.** AI will optimise for what's been built before. Your job is partly to call out "we shouldn't be doing this at all."
- **Reading the room in a meeting.** Politics, body language, the unsaid thing — that's the actual job some weeks.
- **Customer interviews.** The signal is in how they hesitate, what they bring up unprompted, what they don't say. AI summaries flatten this.
- **Saying no.** Sometimes the right answer to a stakeholder is no. AI will help you couch it; you have to own it.

## Three indispensable moves

1. **Keep a "decisions journal" per project.** What you decided, why, what you'd revisit. AI summaries don't substitute for this — but they can help you maintain it.
2. **One-page PRDs, always.** If you can't fit it on a page, you don't understand it yet. Use AI to help you cut, not pad.
3. **Talk to customers regularly without an agenda.** AI doesn't replace this. Five 30-minute conversations a month is the unfair advantage.

## One uncomfortable truth

PM work that was "translation between functions" is shrinking — engineers can ask AI directly, designers don't need a PM to describe what users want. The PM jobs that are growing are the ones tied to clarity of vision, customer relationships, and judgment about what *not* to build. Don't get caught defending the parts AI does well.
