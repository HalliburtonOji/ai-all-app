## Where AI is genuinely useful in this job

- **Transcripts and rough cuts.** Whisper-quality transcripts are now table stakes. Use them to find your selects without scrubbing every clip — search the transcript, jump to the timecode.
- **Caption generation and burn-in.** Auto-captions are good enough that you only edit, not write from scratch.
- **B-roll search.** "Find me 3 clips from this 40-min interview where they talk about pricing" — the model reads the transcript, you cut.
- **Voiceovers for placeholder.** Drop a synthetic VO into a rough cut to test pacing before booking talent.
- **Title-card and chapter-marker drafts.** Pull a structure from the transcript before you sit at the timeline.

## Where AI is not the right tool

- **The cut itself.** Pacing, where to breathe, when to hold — that's craft. Auto-cuts feel auto-cut.
- **Music selection.** Models suggest based on tags. The right music is felt, not tagged.
- **Story shape.** What the piece is actually about. Models will smooth your edges; sometimes the edges are the point.
- **Final colour and audio mix.** Don't trust AI mastering on a paying job.

## Three indispensable moves

1. **Pre-edit with the transcript.** Open the transcript, find your story beats, mark the timecodes — then open the timeline. You'll spend a third less time scrubbing.
2. **Build a sound library on disk.** Music, SFX, room tone. AI is generic; your library is yours. Yours wins.
3. **Templates for repeating formats.** If you cut weekly, pre-build a project template (titles, intro, lower-thirds, end card). Reuse beats reinvent.

## One uncomfortable truth

Auto-edit tools are getting fast at "good enough" cuts. If your value is "I cut things faster than the next person," that floor is rising. If your value is taste — knowing what to cut, what to leave in — that's where AI is least competitive. Move there deliberately.
