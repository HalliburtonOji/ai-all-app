## Where AI is genuinely useful in this job

- **Research before a call.** Pull a prospect's recent news, role history, what their company has shipped. 5 minutes vs 30 of LinkedIn scrolling.
- **First-draft outreach emails.** Specific to that prospect. Not generic. Always rewrite the opening line to actually sound like you.
- **Call recap notes.** Transcribe the call, summarise the action items, draft the follow-up. End your day faster.
- **Objection prep.** Paste a likely objection, ask AI for the strongest counter, then critique the counter — would your champion buy that? Often no.
- **Proposal drafts and pricing tables.** First-draft only. Numbers are yours.

## Where AI is not the right tool

- **The cold open.** Generic AI-written cold outreach is the new spam, and prospects can smell it. The intro line has to be real or you're being filtered.
- **Reading the buyer.** Why they're hesitating, who's actually deciding, what their boss thinks — that's earned in conversation.
- **The relationship after the close.** AI doesn't remember birthdays or that they got promoted. You do.
- **Negotiation.** The model can suggest moves; you read the room.

## Three indispensable moves

1. **Personalise the opening line manually, every time.** The rest of the email can be templated, but the first 1-2 sentences need to be yours and tied to something specific about the prospect.
2. **Keep a "what worked" log.** Which emails got replies, which didn't, against which type of prospect. AI is much more useful when you give it real examples of your wins.
3. **CRM hygiene is now AI-assisted, not AI-replaced.** Make AI summarise the call into the right CRM fields. Don't trust it to fill them right without you reading.

## One uncomfortable truth

Companies are starting to detect AI-written outreach and route it to spam. The path forward is *more* personal, not more volume. If your strategy is "send 200 AI emails a day," the half-life of that strategy is short. Lean into research, real specifics, and warmer intros instead.
