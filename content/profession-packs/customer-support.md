## Where AI is genuinely useful in this job

- **Reply drafts for routine tickets.** Refunds, password resets, "where's my order" — you check, you don't write from scratch.
- **Tone calibration.** Paste a tense draft, ask the model to soften without losing the answer. Useful when you're frustrated and the customer doesn't deserve it.
- **Knowledge-base summarisation.** "What does our policy say about X?" with the doc pasted in is faster than skimming.
- **Translation of tickets.** Especially for support across languages where you're not native.
- **Pattern detection across recent tickets.** "Are 5 different customers complaining about the same shipping carrier this week?" The transcript is searchable.

## Where AI is not the right tool

- **Angry customer triage.** A real person needs to feel heard. AI replies on hot tickets read as evasive even when they're not.
- **Edge cases your KB doesn't cover.** The model will pattern-match to something close and confidently invent. Bad day for the customer.
- **Anything legal, medical, or financial-advice-shaped.** Hand off to humans with the credentials and the liability.
- **Promises about timing.** "It'll arrive by Friday" — only say what you can verify.

## Three indispensable moves

1. **Read every AI draft before sending.** No exceptions. The 5 seconds it takes you saves a 30-minute escalation later.
2. **Keep a personal answer library.** The 30 best replies you've ever written. Paste them as examples when you ask AI for a draft — the tone matches.
3. **Track what actually fixed each ticket.** Not just "resolved" — what the actual root cause was. AI is great at categorising tickets if you give it the inputs.

## One uncomfortable truth

If your job is "answer easy tickets quickly," AI is replacing it — fast. The future of this work is the hard tickets, the angry tickets, the ambiguous ones, the ones that need judgment about what to escalate. Lean toward those. Get good at the parts that AI is bad at, on purpose.
