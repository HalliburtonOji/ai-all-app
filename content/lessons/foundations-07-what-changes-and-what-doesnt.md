## Why this matters

The discourse swings between "AI changes everything" and "AI changes nothing." Both are wrong, in opposite directions. The useful framing is that some things change a lot, some things change a little, and some don't change at all — and which is which depends on the work, not on the headlines.

## What tends to change

- **First-draft anything.** Emails, code, copy, designs, outlines. The empty-canvas problem is mostly solved.
- **Translation, transcription, summarisation.** These are now table-stakes — fast, cheap, and good enough.
- **Lookup-shaped tasks.** "What does X usually mean," "give me 3 examples of Y." Faster than searching.
- **Boilerplate generation.** Standard contracts, standard emails, standard structures.

## What tends not to change

- **Knowing what to make in the first place.** AI can produce. Choosing what to produce is yours.
- **Trust and accountability.** When work goes wrong, a human is on the hook. AI doesn't take blame; it can't.
- **Long-running relationships.** Clients, colleagues, communities. They invest in *you*, not your tool.
- **Taste and judgment.** What's good, what's right, what to ship vs hold. AI flattens these toward an average.
- **Doing the actual hard thing.** When the answer is uncomfortable, AI helps you draft around it. Real work is sometimes saying the uncomfortable thing.

## What changes a little, but not the way you'd think

- **Speed.** People assume AI 5×s your output. Often it 1.3×s — because edit pass time is real.
- **Cost.** Not zero. Predictable, but real. Treat it as a real line item.
- **Quality ceilings.** Floors rise. Ceilings barely move. The best work in any field is still made by humans deeply attuned to it.

## The decision tree

For any task, ask:
1. **Is the output checkable in seconds?** If yes, AI is probably useful. If you can't tell good from bad fast, slow down.
2. **Does this need taste, judgment, or accountability?** If yes, AI assists; you decide.
3. **Is this me-shaped or could anyone do it?** If anyone could, AI raises the floor; if it's you-shaped, AI doesn't reach as far.

## Try it

For one full work day, before each task, pause and silently say which bucket it's in. By evening you'll have a map of where AI compounds for you and where it doesn't. Make decisions from that map.
